# Workflow Findings — groovely

Groovely is the test subject; **product-workflow v1.0.0 is the system under test**.
This file records where the workflow strained, what it failed to catch, and what it
over-specified. It is the meta-deliverable of this project.

Discipline: a surprise surfaced during build is logged here and fed back around the
loop (design → sufficiency-check), never patched in code. See CLAUDE.md Rule 8.

| 1 | verify (design gap) | High | The `verify` agent and its verification-project scaffold are web-shaped: black-box behaviour is driven via Playwright at the URL in `deployment.yaml`, locating elements by role and accessible label. There is no harness for a native iOS/Android binary (would need Appium / XCUITest / Espresso and a different clean-room story). This blocks the stated mobile roadmap at the terminal gate. | Open |
| 2 | discover | Medium | No agent or standard covers third-party API *terms of service* as a spec constraint. The Discogs ToS caching clause (no display of content >6h stale; no caching beyond service need) is a hard constraint on the data model and on what may be persisted, but nothing in the workflow prompts for it. Surfaced only because it was asked about directly. | Open |
| 3 | discover / verify | Medium | Tier 1 requires numeric targets for availability, but `verify` is a one-shot clean-room conformance gate and structurally cannot establish a time-averaged availability figure (e.g. 99.9% monthly). The tolerance rule demands a clause the terminal gate cannot check. Either scope availability out of Tier 1, or route it to a monitoring obligation outside verify. | Open |
| 4 | discover | Low | `BarcodeDetector` browser support was assumed workable and had to be checked externally. No workflow step prompts for platform-capability feasibility of a specified interaction before it enters a feature file. A "capability feasibility" check belongs in discover or design. | Open |
| 5 | verify | High | Verify's black-box harness drives the running app via Playwright at a single URL. Unless the verification project explicitly runs a browser matrix (Chromium/WebKit/Firefox), a cross-browser capability gap — e.g. an interaction built on an API absent in WebKit — passes a green gate while failing for real users. The verify design says nothing about engine coverage. | Open |
| 6 | discover | Medium | The workflow has no artefact for *product principles* — the governing intent a spec must stay consistent with. Principles emerged mid-elicitation (P1–P4) and immediately contradicted an earlier agreed decision (online-only vs "works offline"). Nothing in the `discover` contract prompts for intent before eliciting behaviours, so contradictions surface late and by luck. Recommend a principles step at the front of discover. | Open |
| 7 | discover / standards | Medium | `standards/bdd.md` states "AND is prohibited — if you want AND, split into multiple scenarios" without saying whether it bans the `And` *step keyword* or any *conjunction within a step*. The strict second reading is unsatisfiable for compound-parameter actions (a registration form needs both an email and a password; splitting yields half an action, which is not a boundary action). Every feature file depends on the interpretation. The standard needs one sentence disambiguating it. | **Closed** — `standards/bdd.md` amended: bans the `And` step keyword; a conjunction within a step is permitted only where the joined items are parameters of one action or facets of one outcome. |
| 8 | discover | High | A significant requirement (personnel credits — the product's strongest differentiator) surfaced only when the Product Engineer read a plain-language scope summary, after 34 feature files had been written. The elicitation asked "what are the most important things this system must do?" but never asked what the collector *values* about the objects being catalogued. A domain-immersion step — what makes this collection worth having? — would have surfaced it in the first ten minutes rather than after the artefacts existed. | Open |
| 9 | discover | Medium | The workflow has no mechanism for *removing* a superseded artefact. `purchase-details.feature` was invalidated by review; nothing in the agent contract says whether to delete it, tombstone it, or record its supersession. Left in `_to_delete/` for the Product Engineer. A stale `.feature` file left in place would silently become a spec clause verify must enforce. | Open |
| 10 | discover | High | The workflow defines an Operating Tolerance for *how assertively to challenge* a requirement, but no **admission test** for whether a requirement belongs in scope at all. With none, late "good ideas" are absorbed by default and discovery has no defined end. The obvious rule — "is it outside the defined scope?" — is circular, since scope is constituted by the accepted requirements; it would have rejected this project's strongest requirement. Recommend the workflow require a principles artefact and an explicit admission test anchored to it ("does it positively serve a stated principle?"), authored before behaviours are elicited. | Open — test adopted for this project in product-principles.md |
| 11 | discover / verify | High | Several drafted Then steps were satisfiable by a broken implementation — "the collector is shown that record" passes if search returns the entire collection. Nothing in `standards/bdd.md` or the `discover` scenario critique loop requires a Then step to be *falsifiable by the obvious failure mode*; the observability check only asks whether the outcome is user-visible. The verify design demands checks with teeth, but the spec that seeds them has no equivalent requirement. Recommend adding a falsifiability check to the critique loop. | Open |
| 12 | discover / verify | High | Some of the most security-relevant requirements are **not black-box observable**, yet `standards/bdd.md` mandates observable Then steps and verify's behavioural suite is black-box. Erasure completeness ("no record remains") and EXIF stripping can only be partly established from outside; both had to be restated as weaker observable consequences. The workflow provides no mechanism for a behavioural clause to hand off to verify's white-box/architecture suite, so the stronger assertion is simply lost. Recommend an explicit clause-routing rule: a requirement that cannot be falsified black-box must be traceable to a structural check in the architecture-rules manifest. | Open |
| 13 | discover | Medium | A cut requirement left a stale assertion behind: purchase details were removed from the data model, scope, field map and out-of-scope list, but `view-record.feature` still asserted them. Nothing in the workflow re-validates existing feature files against a changed data model, so a scenario can outlive the field it depends on and would fail the gate for a reason unrelated to any defect. Recommend a spec-consistency check whenever the data model changes. | Open |
| 14 | discover | High | The admission test was applied to *requirements* but never to the *details elaborated from them*. "Search, filter and sort" was admitted as a capability; four filter facets were then enumerated by reflex — filtering by whichever fields happened to exist — without any of them being tested against a principle. One (filter by person) silently duplicated a capability already specified elsewhere (browse-by-person), giving two implementations of one result set for verify to check. Scope creeps below the level the test is watching. Recommend the admission test apply recursively to elaborated detail, not only to top-level requirements. | Open |
| 15 | discover | Medium | Finding #13 recurred immediately and predictably: cutting condition grading left three feature files asserting a field that no longer existed (`grade-condition`, `filter-collection`, `view-record`). It was caught only because a grep for residual references was run by hand. A field removal should mechanically fail until every artefact referencing it is reconciled — the `validate-artefact.sh` hook validates a file's structure on write but never its consistency with the data model. | Open |
| 16 | sufficiency-check | High | The Tier 1 pattern catalogue contains **no falsifiability pattern**. Twenty findings — including "2FA is decorative and the suite still passes" and "erasure leaves every photograph fetchable" — came from a lens added deliberately for this run, not from the prescribed catalogue. A spec can satisfy every catalogue pattern and still be verified entirely by assertions a broken build passes. The `verify` design demands checks with teeth; the gate that certifies the spec feeding it does not. Add falsifiability patterns to the catalogue: vacuous "only" assertions with no cardinality, Givens without distractors, Thens true before the When, absence assertions with no positive counterpart, and every ended authority requiring a follow-up scenario that attempts to use it. | Open |
| 17 | sufficiency-check | Medium | Running the check **out of phase was productive, not merely tolerable** — 46 Tier 1 findings against discover artefacts alone. The contract requires design to be complete first, which means every one of these would have been carried into architecture and ADRs before correction. Recommend the check run at the end of discover as well as after design, with Step 0 phase-scoped rather than all-or-nothing. | Open |
| 18 | sufficiency-check | High | The agent that authored the artefacts is the agent that runs the check on them. Nothing in the contract requires independence, so the default behaviour is marking one's own homework — and the author's blind spots are precisely the defects that survive. This run only worked because independence was arranged deliberately (two reviewers given the bundle and the catalogue but no access to the authoring reasoning). Recommend the contract mandate independent scanners. | Open |
| 19 | discover / sufficiency-check | Medium | Cutting a requirement left its **supporting machinery** standing. Purchase price was removed, but the entire currency apparatus it justified — a supported-currency list, an account-level preferred currency, a per-entry currency column, an ISO 4217 exponent rule and a currency select control — survived unexamined, serving one remaining optional field. This is not a stale *reference* (finding #13/#15, where an artefact points at something deleted) but a stale *dependency*: nothing points at anything missing, so no consistency check detects it, and the surface simply persists. Recommend that removing a requirement trigger an explicit review of what existed only to support it. Surfaced by the Product Engineer, not by the check. | Open |
| 20 | sufficiency-check | High | **Remediation introduced defects at ~17%** (8 new against 46 closed) on a purely textual, non-executable artefact. The `sufficiency-check` contract's rule "ALWAYS re-scan after resolutions" is thereby VINDICATED — this is a point in the workflow's favour, not against it. But the contract does not say the re-scan must be independent of whoever performed the remediation, and the introduced defects are exactly the ones the remediator cannot see: they look correct to the person who just wrote them. Recommend the re-scan be mandated independent, and that a remediation diff be reviewed against the artefacts it touched but did not edit. | Open |
| 21 | sufficiency-check | High | The most dangerous defect class found is **a scenario that asserts the wrong expected value**. `sort-edge-cases.feature` came to demand the opposite of the stated collation rule, so a conforming build FAILS the gate and a defective build PASSES it. This is strictly worse than a toothless assertion: a toothless test fails to catch a defect, whereas an inverted test actively enforces one. Nothing in the pattern catalogue looks for a scenario whose expected value contradicts a spec clause. Recommend a clause-to-scenario consistency check: every scenario asserting a rule must be evaluated against that rule's stated definition, not merely against the BDD format. | Open |
| 22 | discover / sufficiency-check | High | The workflow has **no provisional requirement state**. A requirement is either specified or out of scope; there is no way to record "specified well enough to build, explicitly pending exploratory validation, expected to change". Page size is the exemplar: it cannot be settled on paper because it depends on tile size, thumbnail weight and scroll feel on a real device. Forcing a number into the spec makes it look decided, and every downstream artefact then treats it as settled. This is the Product Engineer's own principle — code is the final stage of specification — with nowhere in the workflow to express it. Recommend a PROVISIONAL marker carrying the question to be answered, the method that would answer it, and a rule that verify checks the stated value while the marker records that the value is not yet earned. | Open |
| 23 | discover | Medium | A stated Product Engineer preference was silently overridden by an option description. The Product Engineer argued for a page size of 10; the multiple-choice option they then selected carried "grid at 24, list at 20" inside its description text, and the change was never surfaced. An option's headline can carry an unrelated decision in its body, and approval of the headline reads as approval of the body. Recommend that any option changing a previously stated preference name that change explicitly in its label, not its description. | Open |
| 24 | sufficiency-check | Critical | **Specification remediation does not converge under hand-editing.** Three independent adversarial scans: 46 findings, then 44 (~8 introduced, 17%), then 50 (~12 introduced, 27%). The rate is rising. One finding recurred with the two documents swapped; another was logged as fixed and never fixed. Root cause: facts are RESTATED across documents rather than REFERENCED, so there is no single place to change and no mechanical check that a change is complete. The workflow hard-gates the build on every slice while the specification feeding it is edited by hand with no gate. Recommend: single source of truth per constant; a consistency script asserting no removed term survives, each constant appears once, scenario arithmetic matches its constants, and glossary terms are used exactly where defined; scenario-to-clause traceability so an inverted scenario is detectable; and edits that fail loudly on a non-match. | Open |
| 25 | sufficiency-check / product-log | Critical | **Two false completion entries were written to `product-log.md`**, the authoritative state artefact. Both came from an edit script that reported success without asserting its match had occurred. `product-log.md` is what the navigator and package agents read to determine project state, so a false entry is not a cosmetic error — it can advance a phase on work that never happened. Recommend that any agent writing a completion entry be required to verify the claimed change by re-reading the artefact, and that the workflow treat an unverified completion entry as a Tier 1 defect in its own right. | Open |
| 26 | discover / standards | Medium | The workflow's `out-of-scope.md` conflates two different lifecycles under unmarked headings: items **deferred** (still valid, may return) and items **abandoned** (vocabulary meaningless, reappearance is a defect). Only the second is mechanically checkable, and only the second should block. Recommend the template split them — `out-of-scope.md` for postponements, `retired-terms.md` for abandonments — since a single list cannot serve both. Surfaced by the Product Engineer when reviewing the consistency-gate design. | **Closed** — implemented in this instance; handed upstream in `UPSTREAM.md`. |
| 27 | sufficiency-check | Medium | A consistency check is only useful if it is **precise**. The first working draft produced 351 findings against a clean specification, almost all false — it scanned logs as though they were specifications, read dates as values, and matched bare common words as retired terms. A check that fires indiscriminately trains everyone to ignore it, which is functionally identical to having no check. Any mechanical gate added to the workflow must be measured for false-positive rate before it is made blocking, and must be shown to have teeth by mutation before a clean result is trusted. | **Closed** — checker rebuilt to zero false positives and mutation-tested; guidance recorded in the `spec-consistency` skill. |
| 28 | discover / standards | High | **A glossary term that tangles two independent axes passes every check the workflow has.** `cover image` named an image by *which face of the sleeve* it showed while actually distinguishing it by *whose picture it was* — the collector's or a lookup's. Set beside `front photograph` and `back photograph` it read as a third face, so the three names looked like siblings when two belonged to one axis and the third to another. Every mechanical check passed: the term was defined, used, unretired and internally consistent. It was caught only because the Product Engineer asked what the difference between the three was — the question a reader asks and a checker cannot. Recommend the glossary standard require each defined term to name the axis it varies on, and the critique loop test sibling terms for orthogonality: if two terms in one family answer different questions, one of them is misnamed. | Open — renamed to `catalogue image` in this instance, with an explicit `display image` precedence rule |
| 29 | verify / tooling | High | **A line-scoped mechanical gate has a whitespace-shaped hole.** The consistency checker matched retired and glossary terms line by line, so a multi-word term wrapped across a line break was invisible to it — a retired term could re-enter a live artefact and pass a *blocking* check purely by falling either side of a fill column, and a live glossary term could be reported as unused for the same reason. Both were latent from the moment the checker was written and neither showed up in the mutation tests, because every injected defect happened to be authored on one line. The general lesson is that a mutation suite proves teeth only against the defect shapes it injects, and a suite authored by the same hand as the checker will inject the shapes the checker already handles. Recommend mutation suites include a formatting-variance axis — the same defect wrapped, indented, emphasised and table-cell-split — for any check that consumes prose. | **Closed** — checker now matches a two-line window for boundary-spanning terms only, skipping windows whose next line carries its own cut-record or pragma exemption; glossary matching normalises whitespace; four mutations added covering wrapped and unwrapped cases |
| 30 | verify / tooling | High | **A mechanical gate punished the behaviour it exists to encourage.** The consistency checker reads numbers out of prose to compare them against the constants registry — and a constant *reference*, `C-043`, carries the digits 043. Cited next to a unit ("C-043 of photographs per record") it was parsed as the value 43 and reported as contradicting a different constant. Referencing a constant by ID instead of restating its number is the single behaviour that most reduces the drift the registry was built to stop, and the checker made it fail the build. `C-056` had escaped the same fate only by luck, having no unit adjacent to it. The general shape is worth carrying upstream: **any check that parses values out of prose must first strip the vocabulary of its own registry**, or the gate taxes correct practice and rewards restating the number. | **Closed** — constant references stripped before number extraction; mutation-tested that a genuinely wrong value sitting beside a reference is still caught |
| 31 | verify / tooling | High | **A constant with no probe is unprotected, and nothing says which constants are protected.** C-040 was added to the registry, cited by four artefacts, and contradicted in a test mutation without the checker noticing — because the probe table that drives the constant check is hand-maintained and had no entry for it. A registry that *looks* authoritative while silently covering only some of its rows is worse than an obviously partial one: every clean run reads as full coverage. Two recommendations. Coverage should be **reported, not assumed** — the checker should be able to state which constants it can actually detect a contradiction in. And probe selection is a real skill worth documenting: the probe must be the phrase that *means* the constant ("longest edge"), never the unit it is measured in ("pixels"), because scenarios legitimately state many values in the same unit and a probe on the unit fires on all of them. | Open — probe added for C-040 and the selection rule recorded in the `spec-consistency` skill; coverage reporting NOT built |
| 32 | discover | High | **An expensive design argument is diagnostic of an unjustified requirement, and nothing in the workflow reads it that way.** The back-sleeve photograph passed the admission test — it served P5, and the reasoning (a jazz sleeve carries the liner notes and personnel) was sound. What followed was a long argument about stored image sizes, liner-note legibility in pixels per inch, thumbnail derivatives, schema surface for a second stored object, and the erasure obligation that second object would carry. The Product Engineer then cut the requirement in one sentence — *Groovely is a catalogue, not a digital replacement for the record itself* — and the entire apparatus dissolved with it, including a PROVISIONAL constant, a three-rung precedence rule and two scenarios written ninety minutes earlier. The admission test asks whether a requirement SERVES a principle. It does not ask whether the cost it drags behind it is PROPORTIONATE to the principle it serves, and the cost is invisible at admission time — it only appears once the implications are worked through. Recommend the workflow treat a design debate that keeps expanding as a signal to re-examine the requirement that spawned it, not merely a problem to solve. The cheapest defect is the one whose requirement is withdrawn. | Open |
| 33 | discover / standards | Low | **A postponed capability is unprotected against SPECIFICATION drift, though not against implementation drift.** Additional images per record were postponed, not retired — correctly, by the Product Engineer's own distinction — so `front photograph`, `back photograph` and `photograph slot` are absent from every artefact and registered in neither registry. **This finding was first logged claiming no gate would fire on a reintroduced back photograph. That was wrong, and the Product Engineer corrected it: out of scope means it is not implemented, and Rule 7 makes that mechanical** — an unrequested stored field or capability fails verify. The implementation case is covered by the terminal gate. What survives is narrower and worth stating precisely: if a future *specification* agent writes a back photograph back into `data-model.md` while remediating something else, it becomes specified, verify then accepts it as requested surface, and the postponement has been reversed with nothing to notice. That is the R-2 regression shape (new prose against an unreconciled decision) applied to an out-of-scope decision rather than to a schema. Recommend no third registry state — the cost is not worth it — but that the sufficiency-check catalogue gain a pattern asking whether any artefact now specifies something `out-of-scope.md` says is postponed. | Open, downgraded from Medium after correction |
